Executive Summary

The Exact Completion Assembly Theorem establishes that the standard analytic completion correction arising in the explicit formula and completed logarithmic derivative can be decomposed exactly into packetwise measures localized to geometric chambers defined by the internal divisor-count structure of prime gaps.

The theorem requires four conditions: explicit-formula compatibility, prohibition of negative zero-radius leakage, strict packetwise localization of negative correction support outside each packet’s geometric maximum excursion, and controlled summability of negative folded costs under the kernel. When these hold, every packet acquires a completion transport radius at least as large as its geometric maximum. This immediately yields packetwise control on drift and aggregate completion cost while ensuring the assembled residual remains compatible with a nonnegative reciprocal-balanced folded measure on the zero-excess plane.

This result is not an incremental refinement of existing analytic machinery. It demonstrates that the global correction terms traditionally supplied by complex analysis (involving the Gamma factor and poles) can be constructed bottom-up from the same local arithmetic geometry that permits direct deterministic reading of the next prime from ordered divisor counts inside individual gaps. The May 2026 documentation push supplies the full supporting apparatus: localization lemmas, term decompositions, transport capacity identities, reservoir allocation rules, global negative-cost conditions, zero-radius constant assignments, drift inequalities, and practical kernel-weighted packet diagnostics.

The unification is profound. Geometric chamber structure and lock-depth transport, previously developed for motif grammar, pruning rules (now at 97), and tier-3 derivation, now carry the analytic completion load without introducing artifacts or requiring classical scaffolding.

Statement of the Theorem

The completed logarithmic derivative decomposes as

Q(s) = R(s) + C_comp(s)

where the standard completion correction is

C_comp(s) = −1/s − 1/(s−1) + (1/2)log π − (1/2) Γ′/Γ(s/2).

Exact Completion Assembly Theorem. The standard completion correction C_comp(s) admits a packetwise decomposition in the centered folded coordinate

η_comp,z = η⁺_{0,z} + Σ_{(p,q)} η_{p,q,z}

such that the following four conditions hold:
  1. Explicit-formula compatibility. The transform of the assembled correction equals C_comp(s) exactly on the common domain of the completed quotient.
  2. No negative zero-radius leakage. The zero-radius completion constants are assigned such that η⁻_{0,z} ≡ 0.
  3. Packetwise localization. For each chamber packet P(p,q) with geometric maximum M_{p,q} = max |x_n| over n ∈ P(p,q), supp(η⁻_{p,q,z}) ⊆ {x : |x| ≥ M_{p,q}}.
  4. Controlled summation. The packetwise negative folded costs are summable on the common folded-kernel domain.

Consequence: When the theorem holds, every packet possesses a completion transport radius ρ_{p,q}(z) ≥ M_{p,q}. The Packet Drift Weighted Average Lemma then implies |D_{p,q}(z)| ≤ ρ_{p,q}(z) R_{p,q}(z). The Aggregate Completion-Cost Bound and Folded Packet Drift Inequality hold packetwise, and the assembled completed residual is compatible with a nonnegative reciprocal-balanced folded measure.
