The Web Around A Semiprime

Main Claim

A semiprime is not isolated. The composites around it carry public traces of the distances to its two factors. When those traces are treated as a web, the factor distance can appear before any direct division of the semiprime is attempted.

Slide 1
A semiprime has more public structure around it than it seems to have.

The center number is visible. Its two factors are hidden.

The nearby composites are also visible. They are the first public objects in the web.

Slide 2
The usual question is direct: which number divides the semiprime?

Conventional factoring asks whether a proposed number divides the center number.

The search is aimed at the hidden factors themselves.

The web method starts somewhere else: with the visible neighborhood around the center number.

Slide 3
The method turns away from the center and looks at the neighborhood.

The public data is not just the center number. The surrounding composites are visible too.

The object of attention changes from one number to a field around that number.

Slide 4
The nearby composites draw threads through the neighborhood.

A thread is a public repeated relationship. It passes through multiple nearby positions.

Several threads crossing the same distance create stronger evidence than one thread alone.

Slide 5
The method lets thread intersections nominate a distance.

The nominated object is a distance from the center that many public threads independently point toward.

Slide 6
The signal does not need both sides to be equally strong.

One side can carry the rich pattern. The other side only has to confirm the same distance.

The useful shape is anchor plus confirmation, not forced symmetry.

Slide 7
A global top list can hide the correct distance.

The winning run showed that the signal was present, but it was inside its own distance band.

Each distance scale needs its own local nominations. One giant ranked list can bury the hit under nearer, denser noise.

Slide 8
On the benchmark surface, the public web nominated a factor distance every time.

Public nominations were frozen first. The hidden factors were used afterward to check the result.

Across the six benchmark cases, one factor distance appeared in the public output every time.

What Was Measured

Six semiprimes were tested. In all six cases, one factor distance appeared in the frozen public web nominations. The hidden factors entered only after the nominations were fixed, as a check against the output.
