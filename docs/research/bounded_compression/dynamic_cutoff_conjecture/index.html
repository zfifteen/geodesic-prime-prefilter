The Dynamic Cutoff Conjecture for GWR/DNI Prime Walks

The main bounded-compression branch asks whether the exact unbounded GWR/DNI gap scan always compresses to the finite local window C(q) = max(64, ceil(0.5 * log(q)^2)).

Headline State

The unbounded DNI/GWR transition is exact by construction. The open theorem target is the bounded compression layer: whether every prime gap's selected interior witness is reached before the dynamic cutoff when scanning from the known right prime q into the next gap.

Three Gates

1. Exact Compare Scan Extension

Push the bounded-vs-unbounded counterexample scan beyond 10^7 while preserving its narrow contract.

  • Input: consecutive right primes in increasing order.
  • Reference: full unbounded GWR/DNI gap scan.
  • Output: first counterexample or certified finite range.

2. Square-Branch Extremal Catalog

Treat prime-square offsets as the dangerous family and record the actual envelope pressure.

  • Track utilization ratio offset / C(q).
  • Record extremal squares and adjacent gap state.
  • Separate retreating, stable, and approaching frontier behavior.

3. Dominant d=4 Proof Attempt

Try to convert the observed square-exclusion and first- d=4 arrival reduction into a theorem or a finite obstruction list.

  • Prove square exclusion for the d=4 selected regime.
  • Prove first interior d=4 dominance after exclusion.
  • If proof fails, isolate explicit obstruction families.

Acceptance Criteria

  • A theorem result states exact hypotheses and proves the dynamic cutoff bound under those hypotheses.
  • A falsification result records the first counterexample with q, next endpoint, witness offset, cutoff, divisor-count class, and square-branch state.
  • Every finite scan reports exact range, row count, maximum peak offset, maximum utilization, and whether the result is measured evidence rather than theorem proof.
  • No classical primality or factorization method chooses the PGS output. Classical checks remain downstream audit only when present.
