Outside-observer note

A Small Sign of Memory in Prime Gaps

We found evidence that one prime gap may carry information about the next one. The effect is real enough to measure, but not yet specific enough to call a solved mechanism.

The Object

Take two neighboring prime numbers. The whole-number space between them is a prime gap. Every number inside that gap is composite.

This project studies the internal pattern of those composite numbers. In each gap, the method selects one interior number, called w. The prime at the right edge of the gap is q.

Now draw a ruler that starts at w and ends at the next prime-square boundary after w. Mark where q lands on that ruler.

The Question

The simple question is not whether prime gaps are random or non-random in a broad philosophical sense. The question is much narrower:

When two current gaps look the same under the known facts, does the one that leads to a particular next-gap state sit in a different place on the ruler?

If yes, the current gap carries a small amount of recoverable information about the next gap. That is what we mean here by memory.

What We Found

The first pooled test found a visible signal. When the selected number had a simple divisor shape, splitting rows into low-ruler and high-ruler groups separated next-gap behavior.

1778 current-gap to next-gap transitions in the pooled test
0.712 next-target share for low-ruler rows
0.655 next-target share for high-ruler rows
0.057 measured separation between the two groups

That was the first sign of memory: a mark inside the current gap separated behavior in the next gap.

The First Correction

We tried a stricter held-out test and it failed to promote the signal. That was surprising, but the failure exposed a design problem.

The raw ruler number shrinks across very large scales. A cutoff learned at one scale does not travel cleanly to another scale. That test was partly asking whether a raw number could be reused across powers, not whether the gap carried memory.

The failed held-out cutoff test did not erase the signal. It showed that the raw ruler coordinate is not portable enough by itself.

The Cleaner Test

We then used a smaller test. No trained cutoff. No cross-scale threshold. No model.

We compared rows only when they already matched on the known current-gap facts. Inside each matched cell, we paired rows whose next gap hit the target state against rows whose next gap did not. Then we asked which row sat lower on the ruler.

152 matched cells with both next outcomes
589 target versus non-target row pairs
+73 signed advantage for the square ruler
+70 signed advantage for plain tail length

The Meaning

The matched-pair test says something real is present: rows whose next gap hits the target state usually sit lower on the current-gap ruler.

But plain tail length, the distance from w to q, almost reproduces the same ordering. The square-ruler result is only three signed wins stronger than that simpler control.

Current status: memory-like ordering is measured. The prime-square boundary has not yet been isolated as the specific mechanism carrying that memory.

What Happens After Residue Matching

A parallel outside check showed that much ordinary gap-to-gap memory is explained by modular residue. So we added a stricter question: after matching the current prime's residue modulo 30, does the square ruler still beat plain tail length?

230 residue-matched target versus non-target pairs
+40 signed square-ruler advantage after mod 30 matching
+33 signed tail-length advantage after mod 30 matching
+7 square-ruler edge over tail length

The square ruler still points in the right direction after residue matching, but the edge is not large enough to promote the mechanism. When we also match the previous gap width exactly, only 35 pairs remain, so that stricter residual test is support-limited.

Why It Matters

If the memory interpretation survives stronger tests, then prime gaps are not just isolated intervals with internal structure. They form a sequence where the current gap carries detectable information about the next one.

That would be a larger claim than "prime gaps contain structure." It would mean prime gaps move through recoverable state.

The current evidence does not prove that larger claim. It gives a precise next target: build a larger residue-matched surface and require the square ruler to beat plain tail length by a meaningful margin.

Bottom Line

Measured The current gap contains a detectable ordering connected to the next gap.
Not Yet Proven The prime-square ruler has not been separated cleanly from the simpler tail length explanation.
Next Test Run the same matched-pair test on a larger residue-matched surface and require the square ruler to beat tail length by a fixed signed-win margin.
