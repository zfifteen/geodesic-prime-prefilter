03 · Laws

What is proved

Three universal pillars stand in the formal proof reference. This page states them in public language, then offers denser mathematics in Deeper panels. None of these pillars is a heuristic, a benchmark slogan, or a probability claim.

Proved · universal
I

Direct next-prime rule

Exact divisor-count traversal after a known prime returns the next prime.

Proved · universal
II

Interior maximizer

The leftmost minimum-divisor interior integer uniquely maximizes the comparison score.

Proved · universal
III

Bounded compression

The selected-witness offset is bounded by a dynamic cutoff at Cramér scale.

Pillar I · Next prime from divisor counts

Proved

Start with a known prime. Walk the integers above it in order. Compute the exact number of positive divisors of each integer. Stop at the first integer that has exactly two positive divisors. That integer is the next prime.

The reason is elementary and exact: an integer greater than 1 is prime precisely when it has two positive divisors, and there is always a least prime larger than the start.

\[ q = \min\{ n > p : \tau(n) = 2 \} \]

The algorithm cannot stop before the true next prime, because every strict interior integer is composite and therefore has divisor count different from 2. It does stop at the next prime, because that prime has divisor count 2.

Pillar II · The interior maximizer

Proved

Inside a gap with at least one composite, look at divisor counts on the interior. Take the smallest divisor count that appears, then the first integer that achieves it. That integer is the unique place where a fixed comparison score is largest.

So the “first lightest load” composite is not a convenient label. It is a proved maximizer of the program’s comparison function.

On nonempty I = {p+1, …, q−1},

\[ w = \min\{ n \in I : \tau(n) = \min_{m \in I} \tau(m) \} \]

is the unique maximizer of

\[ F(n) = \left(1 - \frac{\tau(n)}{2}\right)\log n \]

In zero-excess form, \( F(n) = -E(n) \) with \( E(n) = \bigl(\frac{\tau(n)}{2} - 1\bigr)\log n \).

Pillar III · Universal bounded compression

Proved

The selected witness cannot drift arbitrarily far from the left prime. Its distance from p is bounded by a dynamic cutoff that grows like the square of a logarithm. That is the same scale people associate with Cramér’s envelope, applied here to witness placement.

Boundary · read carefully

This is a proved bound on the selected-witness offset w − p. It is not, by itself, a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the Prime Number Theorem, or every classical formulation of Cramér’s conjecture for raw gap size q − p.

For every consecutive prime gap with nonempty interior,

\[ w - p \le C(q) = \max\bigl(64,\ \lceil 0.5 \cdot (\log q)^2 \rceil\bigr) \]

The square-branch case is closed by the Prime-Square Proximity Theorem: when the witness is a square, the distance from the left prime to the first interior prime square still respects the same cutoff scale.

Finite certified bases complete portions of the analytic argument. They are exhaustive verification surfaces for stated ranges, not substitutes for the universal claim. A Lean 4 formalization is in progress as a machine-checked mirror.

How to speak about these laws

State proved theorems directly. Bound them by their hypotheses. Keep measured generator surfaces and cryptology ladders in separate sentences. Do not convert an audit pass into a new theorem, and do not convert a theorem into “validated so far” language.
