01 · Gaps

What is a prime gap?

Take two primes that sit next to each other in the list of primes. Everything strictly between them is composite. That open stretch is the gap.

A picture you can hold

Start at the prime 89. The next prime is 97. Between them live the composites 90, 91, 92, 93, 94, 95, and 96. That list is the interior of the gap.

The gap is not a void. It is a short hallway of ordinary integers, each with its own divisor pattern. Prime Gap Structure begins by treating that hallway as the main object.

Plain object

Left wall: a known prime p. Right wall: the next prime q. Inside: every integer n with p < n < q.

Gap ruler lab

Bar height tracks divisor count τ

Why this matters for the course

Many stories about primes jump straight to “is this number prime?” That is a candidate question. This course asks a gap question instead: what structure does the interval after a known prime carry, and where does that interval close?

Once you can see the chamber, the rest of the atlas has a place to stand: divisor counts mark the chamber, proved laws order it, and a generator can output the next prime as a clean record.

For consecutive primes p and q, the interior interval is

\[ I = \{p+1,\ldots,q-1\} \]

Every element of I is composite. The program studies ordered structure on I (and on the closed reading that includes the endpoints) rather than treating the composites as disposable rejects in a search loop.
