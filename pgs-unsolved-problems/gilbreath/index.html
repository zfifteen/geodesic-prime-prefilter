Difference grammar

Gilbreath's Conjecture

This track asks whether PGS gap grammar can explain the stability of the leading value under repeated absolute differences of the prime sequence.

status: proof-directed measurement active claim status: unresolved implementation: deterministic probes

Original Problem

Start with the sequence of primes. Repeatedly replace each row by the absolute differences of consecutive entries. Gilbreath's conjecture states that the first entry of every later row is 1.

PGS Translation

Object

The first difference row is the ordered prime-gap sequence. Later rows are repeated absolute-difference reductions of that gap grammar.

Grammar Form

The target becomes a stability law for the leading edge of the PGS gap-word cascade.

Latest Advance

The reset admission pair remains 2,4 on the exact seeded endpoint surface, but the closed reset stop-wall is not a single diff-4 law. The 2026-05-20 probe found closed stop-wall differences 4:22, 6:4, 8:1, and 10:1 across 28 closed positive resets.

The diff-4-only stop-wall shortcut is invalidated. The next theorem obligation is to derive the broader stop-wall alphabet from seeded ordered endpoint-gap grammar after the 2,4 reset admission pair.
