Part A: Plain explanation

Plain language first. No specialized background required.

1. The picture in one paragraph

Imagine two secret numbers that multiply to a big public number N. The research program does not guess those secrets by ordinary trial and error. It builds a public “map” of structure around each side, then checks whether the two sides line up through a simple whole-number division rule called floor transport. When the map lines up tightly, the system can issue a structural certificate. When it does not line up, the system must say unresolved and name the failure honestly.

On one important hard example (about 50 bits wide), the system still cannot close. Recent work got better at naming the failure. The new idea is different: it explains why the failure can keep getting better names while the underlying mismatch barely moves.

2. A kitchen-table analogy

Think of two rulers that should meet at the same mark.

  • Carrier ruler: a reading taken from the middle of one side’s structure.
  • Tail ruler: a reading taken just after a reset point on that side.

For a true matching pair, both rulers land near the same target on the other side. The “pinch width” between those two landings is small.

For the hard false geometry we study, one local check can still look “good enough” because the allowance grows when the structure is already stretched. At the same time, the joint pinch is still too wide. That is like lengthening the tolerance on one measurement because the object is warped, then celebrating that the warped object “passed,” while the two rulers still disagree badly when viewed together.

Same public process on two geometries. True geometry pinches tightly. False geometry stays wide even when one local gate is written generously enough to pass.

3. What the new idea says

The candidate has two linked pieces. Their names are technical, but the meanings are simple.

GISA: the allowance grows with the problem

One important local gate (dual-gap D) sets how far a transported carrier reading may land from its target. That allowance depends on gap sizes. On the hard false example, the lower gap is unusually large, so the allowance gets larger too. The false geometry helps write itself a bigger hall pass.

That is Gap-Inflation Self-Authorization (GISA): the rule that was meant to pressure bad geometry becomes easier exactly when the geometry is more inflated.

RPE: check the joint pinch against the same budget

Even if the local carrier gate holds, look at the combined pinch of carrier image and first-tail image around the upper anchor. Ask a simple question:

Plain residual question Is the joint pinch no larger than the dual-gap budget already used for the local gate?

On the hard false pin, the answer is no: pinch 54 is larger than budget 45 (excess 9). On the true comparison pin, the answer is yes: pinch 21 is inside budget 31.

That is the Reciprocal Pinch Envelope (RPE): the same budget scale used for the local gate should also envelope the joint pinch if the geometry is truly mutual.

5. Why residual names kept changing without solving anything

Over several packages, the same hard pin got better residual labels:

  1. First: “carrier misalignment.”
  2. Then: “first-tail misalignment,” after the dual-gap local gate began to hold.
  3. Then: “joint cell C1T2L1,” which names several weak axes together.

Those renames were useful honesty. They were not discharge of residual force. The joint pinch stayed 54 the whole time. Better labeling is not the same as making the rulers meet.

The breakthrough candidate in one sentence The hard residual is not mainly “we need another residual name.” It is that a local gate can self-authorize on inflated false geometry while the joint pinch still exceeds that gate’s own budget.

6. What success would look like later

  • Not success: inventing a prettier residual code while pinch stays 54.
  • Partial success: public rules that keep rejecting false geometry and still resolve known easy controls.
  • Real residual progress: public rules that shrink the pinch / residual ranks toward true geometry, or cleanly close the hard pin without cheating.
  • Forbidden cheating: secretly using factor tests, or widening a fixed window just to force a green result.

7. What you should not conclude

  • Do not conclude that large RSA numbers are solved.
  • Do not conclude that a new eternal mathematical theorem has been proved.
  • Do not conclude that the 50-bit hard pin is closed.
  • Do conclude that the program has a clearer public diagnosis of residual force, with a testable next step.

Part B: Technical details

Full residual geometry under the PGS-native frame. Status labels remain binding.

1. Frame and non-goals

Required inference frame: 

locked PGS endpoint chain
  -> floor transport through modulus N
  -> reciprocal endpoint / carrier closure checks
  -> modulus-link residual taxonomy
  -> structural certificate or named unresolved state

Forbidden as residual inference: classical primality or factor gates, product closure, gcd selection, random search, and first-tail window widen used to force endpoint emission.

Closed theorem stack (context only; not re-litigated here): direct next-prime rule, GWR interior maximizer, universal bounded compression, Prime-Square Proximity (PROOF.md). Those theorems bound local consecutive-gap structure. They do not by themselves supply modulus-link identity recovery.

2. Public objects

On a modulus-link residual evaluation with both lower and upper chamber-reset certificates present, the public fields used by the live rsa-v3 GWR-carrier stack include:

  • lower.carrier_w, upper.carrier_w
  • lower / upper gap widths g_lo, g_up
  • lower.reset_endpoint, first tail offset after reset, upper.anchor
  • lock offset relative to lower gap
  • floor images floor(N / ·) of public abscissae

2.1 Dual-gap carrier gate (live D)

G      = g_lo + g_up
boundD = max(20, floor(1.2 * G))
T_c    = floor(N / lower.carrier_w)
delta_c = |T_c - upper.carrier_w|     # magnitude form used in ranks / narrative
e_c     = floor(N / lower.carrier_w) - upper.carrier_w   # signed form

Dual-gap D holds when carrier proximity satisfies delta_c <= boundD (implementation details in gwr_carrier_closure.py).

2.2 First-tail channel

L_t    = lower.reset_endpoint + first_tail_offset     # when deadline = tail and tail nonempty
T_t    = floor(N / L_t)
e_t    = T_t - upper.anchor
delta_t = e_t                                         # signed first-tail residual

Live absolute first-tail window remains fixed at [-12, 6] for residual honesty. The 50-bit false pin has delta_t = -22 (hard miss). That window is not widened.

2.3 Pinch and residual ranks (already implemented diagnostics / decision migration)

pinch_S = |T_c - upper.anchor| + |upper.anchor - T_t|

R = (r_carrier, r_tail, r_lock)
cell = C{r_carrier}T{r_tail}L{r_lock}

# rank map (hypothesis constants; pin-anchored)
r_carrier = 0 if delta_c <= 20
            1 if 20 < delta_c <= boundD
            2 if delta_c > boundD
r_tail    = 0 if delta_t in [-12, 6]
            1 if delta_t in [-21, -13]
            2 if delta_t <= -22 or delta_t >= 7
r_lock    = 0 if 2*lock > gap
            1 if weak
            2 if very early

On first-tail sequential residual with cell C1T2L1, decision residual migrates to unresolved_by_joint_cell_C1T2L1. That is residual subclass migration (class B), already packaged 2026-07-14. It is not the novelty claimed here.

3. Candidate law: GISA-RPE

3.1 GISA (Gap-Inflation Self-Authorization)

Claim (hypothesis). The live dual-gap bound 

boundD = max(20, floor(1.2 * (g_lo + g_up)))

is monotone nondecreasing in lower-gap inflation. On false mutual geometry whose lower gap is dilated relative to true mutual geometry, D systematically grants a larger carrier-transport allowance. Consequently the carrier stage can hold on false geometry that remains loose under a fixed tight band, transferring residual force downstream rather than loading it at the carrier predicate.

Interpretation: dual-gap D is not merely “more generous structure.” On this false pin it is a policy that self-authorizes inflated g_lo. Residual cell rank r_carrier = 1 already records “loose under D.” GISA names the bound-form mechanism that produces that rank pattern.

3.2 Non-self-authorizing D′ candidates (hypothesis only)

boundD'_min = max(20, floor(1.2 * min(g_lo, g_up)))
boundD'_up  = max(20, floor(1.2 * g_up))

On the same pins both candidates yield bound 20:

  • false: delta_c = 30 > 20 (carrier stage fails / loads residual)
  • true: delta_c = 14 ≤ 20 (carrier stage holds)

Expected honesty outcome if D′ is instrumented: 50-bit likely returns to a carrier-hard residual or joint cell with higher r_carrier, not automatic endpoint class. Mandatory controls before any implementation claim: 40-bit resolve path, ADV-001 residual honesty, historical false class anti-admission, no first-tail window widen.

3.3 RPE (Reciprocal Pinch Envelope)

Claim (hypothesis). When the deadline=tail residual path applies, true mutual geometry should satisfy 

RPE holds  iff  pinch_S ≤ boundD
excess_RPE = pinch_S - boundD

using the same dual-gap scale already present in live D. RPE is a joint residual coupling, not a single-axis sequential residual name.

Pin boundD pinch_S RPE excess_RPE
50-bit false 45 54 fails +9
64-bit true 31 21 holds −10

Novelty relative to residual cell R: R discretizes three axes into ranks and special-cases cell C1T2L1. RPE states a continuous dual-gap-consistent envelope identity. STEP2 historically discussed compound pinch with ad-hoc thresholds; RPE couples pinch to the live dual-gap budget rather than introducing a second free threshold family.

Second-order prohibition RPE must not be used as an endpoint-class close rule. As a close predicate it risks re-admission of false mutuals with lucky small pinch, global α gaming of boundD, and collapse of multi-axis residual honesty into a single scalar. Correct default use: residual honesty / necessary-geometry subclass / diagnostic ledger field only.

3.4 Companion diagnostic: dual-channel cross-coherence χ

e_c = floor(N / lower.carrier_w) - upper.carrier_w
e_t = floor(N / (lower.reset_endpoint + first_tail_offset)) - upper.anchor
χ   = e_c - e_t

On the committed 50-bit residual deltas: e_c ≈ +30, e_t ≈ −22, hence χ ≈ 52 (large opposite-sign channel errors). χ is not identical to pinch_S: pinch collapses both channels onto upper.anchor, while χ scores signed agreement between carrier→carrier and tail→anchor residuals. Status: hypothesis diagnostic; not a decision residual in code today.

8. Formal statement of the candidate (compact)

GISA-RPE residual force candidate (hypothesis)

Let public dual-gap budget boundD = max(20, floor(1.2(g_lo+g_up))) and joint pinch pinch_S be defined from public certificate fields and floor transport through N. On the named false mutual pin, D can hold while pinch_S > boundD and residual stock (pinch_S, R) remains far from true-close geometry. Residual-code migration under fixed stock is residual re-projection, not residual discharge. A residual-force advance requires public operators that either load residual at non-self-authorizing carrier stages, reduce stock toward true-close class, or close under fixed honesty gates.

9. Open technical uncertainties

  • Two-pin separation risk: RPE and GISA arithmetic currently rest on a thin true/false mutual pair plus 40-bit control.
  • Shared factor 1.2 may be historically fitted to carrier geometry; reusing it for pinch may entangle D and RPE falsification.
  • Lock weakness and profile mismatch may be co-primary residual axes not reducible to excess_RPE.
  • Pinch definition currently double-targets upper.anchor; target-correct alternatives (carrier vs carrier, tail vs anchor) remain open.
  • No residual-family high-scale surface; generality across bit lengths is unresolved.
  • Certificate acquisition failures (128/256 missing lower cert) are a separate constraint family.
