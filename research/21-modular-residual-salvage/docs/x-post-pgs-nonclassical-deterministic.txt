PGS is non-classical and deterministic. Those two facts set what counts as a contribution, a counterexample, a salvage, and a success. They are the evaluation rules.

What classical work usually does

Classical prime work often starts by aiming at primes. It builds candidates. It sieves. It uses residue classes, wheels, modular inverses, Chinese remainder rays, and then certifies with trial up through a square root, or with a primality test. Even when the arithmetic is exact, the research object is a better way to find or protect prime numbers.

That frame is familiar and serious. PGS runs on a different first object and a different success criterion.

What PGS does

PGS starts from ordered prime-gap state.

There is a known prime p. There is a next prime q, or an unresolved state. Between them sits an interior. Inside that interior there are concrete objects you can point at: selected integers, divisor-count structure, a leftmost minimum-divisor witness, modular remainder structure on fixed vectors, chamber boundaries, residual sets, certificates.

The working questions are: what structure is already present in the gap, what named rule acts on that structure, and does the state resolve, stay unresolved, or get invalidated?

That is what non-classical means here.

Non-classical means the first object is gap state. Shared vocabulary leaves that fork intact. The number 210 can appear in classical twin wheels and also appear in a PGS modulus vector. Same integer family. Different job. In classical work it often helps aim endpoints. In PGS it helps read a selected witness already fixed by gap structure. Modular arithmetic stays available. Its role is secondary to gap objects and named rules.

Deterministic means a forced state

A modular formula can be exact and classical at the same time. Residue adjustment is a good example. Every local root class is exact. The Chinese remainder gluing is exact. The later trial through the square root is exact. That package still sits in classical prime construction.

In PGS, deterministic means the rule forces a state:

resolved,
unresolved,
or invalidated.

Allowed outputs stay inside that triple. Likelihood language stays outside the spine. Candidate search stays outside the spine. Random fallback stays outside the spine. Audit verifies after generation. Audit leaves the choice of q alone.

If the named rule leaves the next prime open, the honest output is unresolved. That is the contract.

Deterministic here means the rule either forces the answer or explicitly refuses to invent one. Careful modular arithmetic is welcome. By itself it is a weaker bar than a forced gap-state rule.

Why this affects how you evaluate PGS

Classical scoring misreads the program at almost every checkpoint.

A better candidate ray looks like progress under classical scoring. Under PGS scoring, candidate rays sit outside the spine.

Trial-to-square-root certification looks like the finish line under classical scoring. Under PGS scoring, that step is audit or comparison. The selection rule lives earlier, on gap state.

Shared modular language looks like the same research program under classical scoring. Under PGS scoring, reading a gap object and manufacturing prime endpoints remain different programs that can use overlapping words.

A salvage that restores “likely primes near dense composites” looks like continuity under classical scoring. Under PGS scoring, likelihood belongs in classical observation only when labeled that way. Likelihood is outside the PGS output types.

One constructed twin pair looks like generator evidence under classical scoring. Under PGS scoring, the generator contract is: input known prime p, output next prime q, or unresolved. Evidence surfaces are ordered runs under that contract, with status labels kept separate: theorem, measured, audit, hypothesis, unresolved, invalidated.

Disproving an over-strong universal lock looks like a program kill under classical scoring. Under PGS scoring, invalidating a bad universal claim is healthy maintenance. The modular lemma on the fixed vector can still stand. GWR can still stand. The next-prime theorem can still stand under its hypotheses. Status separation is part of the method.

How to evaluate it correctly

Ask these questions in this order.

1. What is the first object? Candidate primes put the work in classical scoring. Gap state puts the work in PGS scoring.

2. What is the named gap object or invariant? Endpoint, interior, selected witness, remainder vector, residual set, certificate, and so on.

3. What rule is being applied? State it in forced-state language.

4. What forced outcome does the rule claim? Resolved next prime, unresolved, or invalidated.

5. Where does classical machinery sit? Inference role is outside the spine. Audit and comparison after the fact are allowed when labeled that way.

6. Does the claim match its status label? Theorem only if proved under stated hypotheses. Measured only for exact tested regimes. Hypothesis until forced. Invalidated stays invalidated.

A proposal that fails those questions can still be interesting classical number theory. Entry into the PGS spine requires a gap-first forced-state rule.

A concrete contrast

Classical residue adjustment says: move divisor roots into parameter space, choose safe classes, compress them into a ray, then certify survivors.

That can produce real primes. It can be clean. It can serve as comparison literature.

PGS says: start from the ordered gap, read the structure that is already there, apply the named rule, and return only a forced state.

One program aims. The other reads and forces.

Bottom line

PGS is non-classical because its first move is gap structure.
PGS is deterministic because it advances on rules that force resolved, unresolved, or invalidated.

Evaluate it on those terms. Score it as a cleverer sieve, a better twin-ray factory, or a modular likelihood engine, and serious classical help will keep missing the transfer. Effort can be real while the first object and the success criterion stay misaligned.

Classical modular work can continue on its own track. That work and PGS are different programs, and the difference is load-bearing.
