Hidden Seeds In The Cognitive Distortion Layer

CDL did not leave one idea behind. It left a seed bank. The strongest seed became PGS: exact divisor-count normalization makes every prime a fixed point and every composite a contraction. The remaining seeds are nearby, concrete, and testable.

Source inspected: `zfifteen/cognitive-distortion-layer` at commit `65fbc27`. Local continuity note: `research/00-index/continuity/cdl_seed_deep_dive_2026-05-11.md`.

The Common Root

Begin with an integer, count its divisors, and weight that count by the size of the integer. CDL called that quantity curvature:

kappa(n) = d(n) ln(n) / e^2

The forward normalization was:

Z(n) = n / exp(v kappa(n))

At `v = e^2 / 2`, the expression becomes the exact divisor-weighted power transform:

Z(n) = n^(1 - d(n)/2)

That cancellation is the root. Primes have divisor count `2`, so they land exactly at `Z = 1`. Composites have divisor count greater than `2`, so they fall below `1`.

The Seeds

1 window threshold geometry

A local window can have a closed-form prime ceiling.

In a range `[a,b]`, the prime band reaches its highest point at the right edge because every prime has `d(p)=2`. The proposed threshold is therefore the prime ceiling at `b`.

Mechanism If `a > sqrt(b)`, then new composites inside the window cannot sit below the prime ceiling unless they carry a small factor from before the window. tau_window = 2 ln(b) / e^2
hypothesis CDL concept note needs PGS-native proof

This seed asks whether threshold selection is not training at all, but a geometric consequence of the window bounds.

2 divisor-family bands

Numbers with the same divisor count ride the same rising sheet.

The fixed threshold story failed because it treated all scale as one flat comparison. CDL's formula says something sharper: each divisor-count family has its own moving band.

Mechanism For fixed `d(n)=k`, curvature is forced onto a logarithmic line. kappa_k(n) = k ln(n) / e^2
exact formula threshold falsification evidence band dynamics remain open

Primes are the `k=2` sheet. Prime squares are the `k=3` sheet. Semiprimes and prime cubes are on `k=4`. Richer composites climb higher.

3 prime-square and semiprime envelopes

The composites closest to the prime fixed point are structured boundary cases.

Under the sweet-spot transform, primes sit at `Z=1`. Composites below them do not form a vague cloud. Prime squares make the upper composite envelope because `d(p^2)=3` gives the weakest composite contraction.

Mechanism Prime squares contract as `1/sqrt(n)`. Semiprimes and prime cubes contract as `1/n`. Higher divisor counts fall faster. d(p^2)=3, d(pq)=4, d(p^3)=4
exact divisor classes 100000-run separation envelope theory open

This seed is a boundary theory for the top of the composite field beneath the prime fixed point.

4 level-set theory

Each divisor count defines a power-law ruler.

The sweet-spot transform breaks into a family of curves indexed by divisor count. That turns the integer line into a stack of power-law level sets.

Mechanism Freeze `d(n)=k`, then the normalized score follows a single exponent. Z_k(n) = n^(1 - k/2)
exact family form distribution theory open

The open question is how integers populate these curves, how mass shifts by divisor class, and which level sets control gap interiors.

5 restricted multiplicative structure

The transform is not multiplicative, but it may obey useful restricted inequalities.

CDL's draft noted that `Z` mixes multiplicative factor structure with an exponent controlled by divisor count. That hybrid form does not reduce to standard multiplicativity.

Mechanism For coprime `a` and `b`, divisor counts multiply: `d(ab)=d(a)d(b)`. The exponent of `Z(ab)` therefore changes by a product, not a sum. Z(ab) = (ab)^(1 - d(a)d(b)/2)
exact coprime divisor law inequality seed not yet a PGS law

The seed is to find narrow inequality regimes where this non-multiplicative behavior still gives a deterministic comparison tool.

6 chamber reset relation

CDL band geometry may explain why PGS gaps reset around selected witnesses.

PGS proves that the selected interior integer is the leftmost interior integer with the minimum divisor count, and that it uniquely maximizes the log score. CDL supplies the band geometry behind the same score.

Mechanism Inside a prime gap, all interiors are composites. The first lowest divisor-count interior is closest to the prime baseline under `log Z`. log Z(n) = (1 - d(n)/2) ln(n)
PGS theorem foundation mechanism bridge open

The seed is a bridge: CDL bands describe the score surface, while PGS chamber resets describe endpoint-local structure.

7 fixed-point transport

The sweet-spot cancellation may transport into endpoint-chain or modulus-link coordinates.

The exact cancellation `v=e^2/2` locks primes to `Z=1`. The open question is whether that cancellation can be carried through PGS-native endpoint chains or modulus-link residuals without importing classical factor gates.

Mechanism A fixed prime baseline gives a coordinate that survives scale. A transported coordinate would preserve the baseline while moving through endpoint state. fixed point -> endpoint chain -> residual state
sweet-spot identity transport seed unresolved for modulus work

This is the cryptology-adjacent seed with the sharpest boundary: no `gcd`, no hidden factor gates, no classical replacement for PGS inference.

8 three-regime curvature distinction

Exact integer curvature, smooth average curvature, and recovered traversal rate are different objects.

CDL grew into continuous and cognitive-style layers. Those layers are useful, but they must not be mistaken for exact integer structure.

Mechanism Exact `d(n)` gives the integer field. Average-order `kappa_smooth(x)` gives a continuous surrogate. Recovered `v` measures traversal behavior under a stated support. exact field != smooth surrogate != observer parameter
state separation v recovery benchmarks external data unresolved

This seed is methodological: a future result should name which regime it lives in before claiming what it shows.
