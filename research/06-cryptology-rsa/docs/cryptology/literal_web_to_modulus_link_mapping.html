The Relaunch Objective

The "Literal Multiplicative Web" (inspired by Japanese thread geometry) successfully identified prime factors by observing intersecting small-prime threads at factor distances. However, the requirement to explicitly map an array of size 6 * p rendered it computationally equivalent to trial division ($O(\sqrt{N})$).

This document formalizes the mapping of that geometric phenomenon back into native Prime Gap Structure (PGS) mathematics, transforming an $O(\sqrt{N})$ array-scanning algorithm into a deterministic, constant-scale Modulus-Link Residual calculation.

1. The Observable Object

In the Literal Web experiment LWM-CROSS-01, we mapped public factor threads (small primes like 2, 3, 5) originating from composites near the semiprime $N = p \cdot q$. The critical finding was that the true factor distance offsets ($+p$ and $-p$) were overwhelmingly supported by Cross-Family Threads:threads originating from both the left side of $N$ and the right side of $N$.

We can clearly see why this happens by looking at the values at these exact hole offsets:

  • At hole offset $+p$, the value is N + p = p*q + p = p(q + 1)
  • At hole offset $-p$, the value is N - p = p*q - p = p(q - 1)

2. The Ordinary Mechanism (Why the Threads Cross)

Because $q$ is a prime number (and $q > 3$), it is wedged tightly between two highly composite numbers: $q-1$ and $q+1$.

By the fundamental properties of primes, adjacent numbers $q \pm 1$ are guaranteed to absorb massive amounts of small prime divisibility. For instance, $q+1$ is always even (absorbing the thread for 2). Either $q-1$ or $q+1$ is heavily divisible by 3 (absorbing the thread for 3).

When the visual geometry experiment demanded that threads from the left and right "cross" at $p$, it was unknowingly demanding that $p(q-1)$ and $p(q+1)$ contain small prime factors. It worked perfectly because they always do.

3. The Project Term: Modulus-Link Transport

In PGS-native vocabulary, when a thread (a small prime factor $r$) originates at a composite $N - t$ and lands exactly on $N + p$, it is exhibiting Modulus-Link Floor Transport.

The visual array was physically tracking the transport of divisibility through the modulus $N$. The "Cross-Family Coherence Bonus" that successfully eliminated all noise in the ladder rungs was actually the signature of the Divisor-Count Field of $q \pm 1$ being projected across $N$.

4. Formal Definition (Algebraic Decoupling)

We no longer need to allocate a physical $O(\sqrt{N})$ array to find where the threads cross. Instead, we can algebraically calculate the crossing points using the established PGS invariants.

N = p · q
q ± 1 ≡ 0 (mod r) for selected small primorial components r ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7}

Because $N$ is known, we can directly compute the modulus-link residuals of $N$ with respect to the primorial bases. The geometry of the Literal Web is mathematically identical to solving for the specific offset $p$ where the transported residuals satisfy the highly-composite signature of $q \pm 1$.
