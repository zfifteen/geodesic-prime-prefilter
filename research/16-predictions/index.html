Opening Object

Start with a known prime p. The integers from p+1 onward carry an exact, ordered divisor-count field. The first integer that returns divisor count exactly 2 is the next prime q. Between p and q lies a finite chamber of composites whose divisor counts are completely known once the endpoints are fixed.

The Predictions track studies how the already-proved local objects and invariants inside one such chamber — the divisor-count field, the Divisor Normalization Identity (DNI), the Leftmost Minimum-Divisor Rule (GWR), endpoint chains, modulus links, reciprocal transport, and chamber-reset geometry — can be used to resolve future chamber states, subsequent endpoints, or structural certificates exactly, without classical search or probabilistic models.

PGS Predictions Entry Frame (Mandatory for All Work)

PGS objects → PGS invariants (DNI, GWR, endpoint chain, modulus link) → Named PGS rule or law → resolved / unresolved / invalidated PGS state

Never begin from candidate primality testing, Miller-Rabin, isprime, nextprime, sieves, gcd, divisibility selectors, product closure, factor APIs, or probabilistic assumptions. Classical methods are allowed only in downstream audit or explicit legacy-comparison roles, physically and semantically separate from inference.

Shape Guardrails (Early-Warning Language)

  • Reviving legacy "predictor" framing or the z_band_prime_predictor directory as active inference machinery.
