**After-Action Report: RH-Bridge Research Loop (Grok as Research Director)**

**1. What went well**

- The directive -> short focused note -> formal ACCEPT/REVISE with "exact
  edit required" language created exceptional intellectual hygiene. It
  repeatedly forced Codex to stay inside the precise ask and prevented the
  four failure modes explicitly called out in AGENTS.md.
- A long, technically demanding conditional reduction chain was executed
  successfully on the leading endpoint counterterm, reducing it through five
  distinct steps to a single, well-isolated global arithmetic statement
  (Divisor-Channel Age Orthogonality) with a documented implication chain at
  every stage.
- Consistent, rigorous separation of proof states was maintained: local proved
  PGS facts (GWR selector, chamber structure, zero-excess endpoints from
  PROOF.md) were cleanly distinguished from conditional reductions and from
  the unresolved global recurrence bound.
- The research/12-rh-bridge/README.md served as an effective living navigation
  and status surface, accurately tracking the evolving chain.
- Notes remained short and narrowly scoped. The process successfully resisted
  the common temptation to produce ever-larger documents.
- The acceptance-check format surfaced precise technical issues (e.g., dyadic
  placement arguments) quickly rather than allowing them to accumulate.

**2. What did not go well**

- The loop became almost entirely serial and focused on the leading term of
  one route (reciprocal endpoint counterterm). Other necessary components of
  the full finite-part packet identity (interior prime-power remainder law,
  gamma match, exact assembly compatibility) received little attention in this
  phase.
- The high precision bar, while necessary, generated repeated REVISE cycles
  even on notes that were conceptually correct, creating friction and slowing
  momentum.
- The growing stack of interdependent notes made global state increasingly
  expensive to reconstruct. A new participant (or a future Grok session with
  limited context) would need to read a long chain of documents to understand
  the current frontier.
- The research-meeting / after-action mechanism was invoked only after a long
  sequence of cycles rather than at natural inflection points, missing
  opportunities for earlier course correction.
- Minor but recurring technical looseness appeared in dyadic shifting
  arguments (most notably the "endpoint separation p(q) > q/2" claim), showing
  that the model is still vulnerable to small gaps in rigor when moving between
  scales.

**3. What could be improved**

- Introduce lightweight, persistent "gate ledger" artifacts (a single focused
  markdown or self-contained HTML table) that track all open sub-obligations
  for the full finite-part packet identity / Chamber-Deconvolved Reciprocal
  Balance Lemma at once, not just the currently drilled route.
- Mandate an explicit "What this note does not advance / does not prove"
  paragraph in every strategy or reduction note.
- After every 4-5 successful ACCEPTs on a major chain, require a short "chain
  integrity and visibility" review before continuing the drill-down.
- Add limited parallel tracking: while the primary agent executes the current
  gate, a secondary lightweight track can explore whether alternative routes
  to the same gate (or to the full identity) are shorter.
- Use the research-meeting skill more frequently and at smaller scale (e.g.,
  after every major reduction milestone) rather than only at the end of long
  arcs.
- Consider a lightweight convention for "assumption hygiene" when dyadic or
  scale-shifting arguments are used, so small technical points are surfaced
  earlier.

critical piece of the finite-part packet identity) has been reduced, through a

$$
A_d(X) \ll {X\over d}(\log X)^B

$$
2 \le d \le \sqrt{2X},
$$

in the dyadic interval.

All prior steps (Age-Divisor Energy Bound, Zero-Excess Return Square-Moment

No unconditional progress on the Chamber-Deconvolved Reciprocal Balance Lemma
