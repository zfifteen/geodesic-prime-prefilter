## Current Best Candidate Allocation Rule (with all "if" conditions explicit)

**PGS-native opening frame (binding per PGS Guardian audit and AGENTS.md in the ledger):** The ordered prime-gap state is partitioned into chambers delimited by consecutive prime endpoints p < q. Each chamber contains the packet P(p,q) of structural points n with associated local centered coordinates x_n. The divisor-count field τ(n) on the packet points, together with the Leftmost Minimum-Divisor Rule (GWR), selects the ordered structure and the zero-excess coordinate inside the packet. Dirichlet deconvolution of the chamber-load by the divisor-count series produces the deconvolved coefficients λ(n) = Λ(n) on the packet. The packet drift is the PGS object D_{p,q}(z) = ∑_{n∈P(p,q)} λ(n) J_z(x_n) in the folded kernel at scale z. The packet excursion radius is the PGS object M_{p,q} = max |x_n| over the packet (satisfying M_{p,q} < 1/2 by Bertrand properties in the strategy documents). A chamber/packet carries unresolved PGS state when its drift D_{p,q}(z) is nonzero and unmatched by completion transport. The candidate explicit algorithm for the packetwise measures η_{p,q,z} is the deterministic proportional opposite-sign allocation rule applied to the transport reservoir (atoms of centered radius ≥ 1/2). This rule uses only PGS packet objects and invariants (localization M_{p,q} < 1/2 < reservoir radius; reciprocal transport in the folded kernel; GWR ordering survival). It achieves ∫ J_z dη_{p,q,z} = −D_{p,q}(z) exactly when the Transport Capacity Balance Identity holds. Classical completion expressions appear only as the downstream exact-match verification target on the tested regime.

**Reservoir atom computations required by the candidate allocation algorithm (from Analyst assessment of classical completion terms; supplied as input to PGS packet allocation only):** The transport reservoir consists of the pole-pair and trivial-zero atoms (y_j, a_j(z)) with |y_j| ≥ 1/2. For each such atom the candidate algorithm must compute the folded kernel value J_z(y_j) = y_j / (z + y_j²) and the signed odd capacity O_j(z) = a_j(z) J_z(y_j). The signed aggregates T_+(z) and T_-(z) are formed by summing the positive and negative O_j over the reservoir. 

- Pole-pair atoms are finite and explicit: centered positions at y = ±1/2 with masses yielding O contributions of magnitude 1/(2(z + 1/4)) on each side (exact formulas from decomposition of the pole terms in C_comp; classical computation only).
- Trivial-zero transport atoms are at y_m = −2m − 1/2 (m ≥ 0) with masses a_m derived from the digamma expansion of the Gamma factor in C_comp (raw O_m(z) < 0 for all m; one-sided negative odd capacity in the raw representation).
- Gamma regularization constants (from the digamma expansion) and main-term constants sit at zero radius; they contribute J_z(0) = 0 and are assigned exclusively to the global nonnegative folded background η^+_{0,z} (η^-_{0,z} ≡ 0). They form no part of the transport negative budget or odd capacities.

On any finite regime under test, the algorithm uses the explicit pole-pair formulas plus a truncation to the first N trivial-zero atoms (with chosen regularization for the tail) to obtain concrete O_j(z) and T±(z) values. These pair with PGS-derived D_{p,q}(z) and M_{p,q} (computed from chambers, packets P(p,q), GWR selectors, τ field, and deconvolved λ on the same finite set).

**Base objects (candidate construction, PGS objects first):** 
- PGS packet objects: chambers, packets P(p,q) = {q} ∪ {interior prime powers in (p,q)}, divisor-count field τ(n), GWR-selected positions and zero-excess coordinate, deconvolved λ(n), packet drifts D_{p,q}(z), packet excursion radii M_{p,q} < 1/2.
- Signed packet demands: P_+(z) = {(p,q) : D_{p,q}(z) > 0}, D_+(z) = ∑_{P_+} D_{p,q}(z); P_-(z) and D_-(z) defined symmetrically.
- Transport reservoir atoms with computed O_j(z) as above; signed capacities T_+(z), T_-(z) after chosen regularization of the infinite trivial-zero part.

**Candidate construction steps (all "if" conditions explicit):**

1. **Net finite-part packet balance precondition (candidate construction):**  
   If the Packet Net Finite-Part Balance Lemma holds in the chosen regularization, i.e.,  
   F.p. ∑_{(p,q)} D_{p,q}(z) = B^{fp}_{comp}(z)  
   (equivalently D_+(z) − D_-(z) = −B^{fp}_{comp}(z)),  
   then the global odd balance is available in finite-part form.

2. **Raw reservoir finite-part capacities (candidate construction):**  
   Compute T^0_+(z) and T^0_-(z) from the pole-pair atoms (explicit finite formulas yielding 1/(2(z + 1/4)) each side) plus the chosen finite-part regularization of the raw trivial-zero atoms (y_m = −2m − 1/2, m ≥ 0). The finite part fixes the difference: T^0_-(z) − T^0_+(z) = B^{fp}_{comp}(z). Gamma regularization constants are assigned outside the transport reservoir (zero-radius nonnegative background only).

3. **Common deficit sign check (candidate construction):**  
   Define Δ(z) := D_-(z) − T^0_+(z).  
   If Δ(z) ≥ 0 (equivalently the symmetric statement for the other side), then a nonnegative common deficit exists.

4. **Symmetric trivial-zero split to close sidewise capacities (candidate construction):**  
   If the net finite-part packet balance and Δ(z) ≥ 0 both hold, then introduce a sign-regularized split on one or more trivial-zero atoms (nearest-atom candidate uses m=0 at |y|=1/2; proportional or gamma-finite-part candidates use distributions across m):  
   For chosen atom(s), set α_m(z) ≥ 0 such that ∑ α_m(z) |J_z(y_m)| = Δ(z).  
   This augments both sides:  
   T_+ = T^0_+ + Δ(z) = D_-(z),  
   T_- = T^0_- + Δ(z) = D_+(z).  
   Exact assembly is preserved atom-by-atom because the signed split on each atom sums to the original δ_{y_m}.  
   Localization is preserved because |y_m| ≥ 1/2 > M_{p,q} for every packet (PGS invariant).  
   The introduced negative folded cost ∑ α_m K_z(y_m) is controlled when the α distribution satisfies the summability condition in the folded kernel.

5. **Proportional opposite-sign packet allocation (candidate construction):**  
   If the sidewise capacities now satisfy T_-(z) = D_+(z) and T_+(z) = D_-(z), then for every transport atom j (after split):  
   - For (p,q) ∈ P_+(z) and negative-capacity shares of j: θ_{p,q,j}(z) = D_{p,q}(z) / D_+(z).  
   - For (p,q) ∈ P_-(z) and positive-capacity shares of j: θ_{p,q,j}(z) = |D_{p,q}(z)| / D_-(z).  
   All other θ = 0.  
   The packetwise measure receives  
   η_{p,q,z} := ∑_j θ_{p,q,j}(z) · (signed transport mass from j, including any α-split contributions).  
   This yields ∫ J_z(x) dη_{p,q,z}(x) = −D_{p,q}(z) exactly (drift cancellation).  
   The negative part η^-_{p,q,z} is supported only on the allocated atoms with |y_j| ≥ 1/2, hence supp(η^-_{p,q,z}) ⊆ {|x| ≥ 1/2} ⊇ {|x| ≥ M_{p,q}} (localization PGS invariant).  
   Controlled summation follows from the kernel summability of the (regularized) transport reservoir when the α split is controlled.

6. **Background assignment (candidate construction):**  
   The zero-radius regularization constants (γ/2 terms, 1/(2(m+1)) harmonic counterterms, etc.) and any unallocated surplus (if Δ < 0 in some regularization) are assigned exclusively to the global nonnegative folded background η^+_{0,z}. No negative folded cost is created at zero radius: η^-_{0,z} ≡ 0.

**Resulting candidate assembly:**  
η_{comp,z} = η^+_{0,z} + ∑_{(p,q)} η_{p,q,z}  
holds atom-by-atom on the transport reservoir (plus background), satisfying the four conditions of the Exact Completion Assembly Theorem *conditionally on the two preconditions in steps 1 and 3*.

The explicit algorithm is therefore: from PGS objects (chambers, packets P(p,q), GWR, divisor-count field, deconvolved λ, D_{p,q}(z), M_{p,q}), compute the packet drifts and demands; from the Analyst-supplied classical formulas compute the reservoir atoms y_j, a_j(z), O_j(z) = a_j(z) J_z(y_j) and T±(z) (pole explicit + truncated trivial zeros with regularization); test the two "if" preconditions; if both hold, perform the symmetric split with a chosen normalization rule for the α_m(z), then execute the proportional allocation to produce the η_{p,q,z} measures.

**Implementation sketch for the allocation (from Numerics MVH plan, candidate construction under test on finite regime only):** The concrete functions compute_drift_demands(packets, z), build_transport_reservoir(z, pole_pair_atoms, trivial_zero_sample), and candidate_allocate(packets, z, reservoir_atoms) implement exactly the proportional opposite-sign rule. They take precomputed PGS packets (with D(z) and M) and a reservoir model (list of (y_j, a_j(z)) with |y_j| ≥ 1/2). They return per-packet allocation shares and observed (T_pos, T_neg, D_pos, D_neg) for imbalance diagnostics. The resulting η_{p,q,z} for each packet is the signed measure assembled from the weighted atoms. This preserves exact assembly (thetas per atom sum to 1 on the allocated side) and non-negative weights by construction. Full code appears in the NUMERICS ledger entry.

**Current status of this candidate rule:** Candidate construction. The proportional allocation rule itself (step 5) and the sketch functions are deterministic once capacities match. The symmetric split (step 4) is a candidate family of normalizations; the gamma-finite-part balance normalization is the strongest but reduces to the Transport Capacity Balance Identity itself. All steps remain conditional on the open identities. The infinite trivial-zero reservoir case and the sidewise Transport Capacity Balance Identity remain fully open.
