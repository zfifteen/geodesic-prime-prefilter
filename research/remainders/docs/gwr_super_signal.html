1. The Blind Spot in Classical Number Theory

In classical analytic number theory, primes are typically found by sieving (crossing out composites) or modeled probabilistically (e.g., Cramér's model, Prime Number Theorem). The space between primes:the prime gap:is treated merely as distance.

Classical theory understands the divisor function d(n), and it understands divisibility by primorials (2, 3, 5, 7, 30, 210, etc.), but it does not use them to structurally map the interior of a specific gap. The discovery of the GWR Super-Signal demonstrates that when you cross-reference the local minimum of the divisor field against its modular primorial resonance, the gap interior becomes an active, deterministic predictor of the next prime.
