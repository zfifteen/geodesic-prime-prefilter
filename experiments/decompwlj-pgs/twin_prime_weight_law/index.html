Research Findings Note

The Twin Prime Weight Law

Deterministic Classification of Twin Prime Gaps under the Integration of decompwlj and Prime Gap Structure

Strongest Supported Claim: Every twin prime pair (p, p + 2) starting above 11 is deterministically locked into the "weight" class of the Prime Gap Structure with a decompwlj weight value of exactly k = 3. This mathematical necessity is proved using elementary modular arithmetic.

1. Finding

Twin primes are pairs of prime numbers that sit right next to each other, separated by only a single even number. Examples include (11, 13), (17, 19), and (29, 31). In our research program, we study how these gaps look when we analyze their divisor complexity, which is how many divisors the numbers inside the gap have.

We classify prime gaps into two main styles based on how they divide up the numbers around them:

  • Weight Class: Gaps where the numbers inside are influenced by small prime divisors.
  • Level Class: Gaps that behave more independently, usually because they only have large divisors.

When running experiments across 50,000 primes, we noticed something strange. Almost every single twin prime gap ended up in the "Weight Class", while the "Level Class" had zero twin primes (with the only exception being the very small pair of 5 and 7).

We solved the mystery of why this happens, and it turns out to be a mathematical rule. If you look at three odd numbers in a row, like 15, 17, and 19, one of them must be divisible by 3. If you have a twin prime pair like 17 and 19, they are both primes, so they cannot be divisible by 3. That means the number right before them (which is 15 in this case) has to be divisible by 3.

Because the number right before them is always divisible by 3, the math that classifies the gap is forced to choose 3 as the key divisor. This low divisor number automatically pushes every single twin prime pair above 11 into the Weight Class. It is an absolute rule, not a statistical guess.

2. Details

In the unified framework combining the decompwlj weight formulation with the Prime Gap Structure (PGS), we establish a deterministic theorem characterizing the algebraic classification of consecutive prime pairs with gap size 2.

Mathematical Definitions

Let p and q be consecutive primes with gap g = q - p = 2. Let the offset interval be I = (p, q), containing the solitary even integer p + 1. The auxiliary offset index is defined as:

ell = p - g = p - 2

Under the decompwlj protocol, the weight k is the minimal positive divisor of ell that satisfies the boundary condition:

k > g

Since g = 2, this simplifies to finding the smallest divisor of ell that is strictly greater than 2. The partition boundaries classification assigns the gap to one of two structural regimes:

Level Class

Characterized by high-scale modular thresholds:

k^2 > ell

Weight Class

Characterized by sub-square modular containment:

k^2 ≤ ell

Theorem: The Twin Prime Weight Law

For all consecutive prime pairs (p, q) with gap g = 2, if p > 11, then the gap classification is deterministically constrained to the Weight Class with a localized weight parameter k = 3.

Proof

Step 1: Divisibility of the preceding odd index. Let p > 5 be the lower prime of a twin prime pair, meaning both p and p + 2 are prime. Consider the sequence of three consecutive odd integers {p - 2, p, p + 2}. In any sequence of three consecutive odd integers, exactly one element is congruent to 0 modulo 3.

Step 2: Constraint on the primes. Since p and p + 2 are prime and strictly greater than 3, we have:

p ¬≡ 0 (mod 3)
p + 2 ¬≡ 0 (mod 3)

By elimination, this requires:

p - 2 ≡ 0 (mod 3)

Step 3: Minimum divisor determination. Since ell = p - 2 and ell ≡ 0 (mod 3), the set of divisors of ell includes 3. Because p > 5, we have ell > 3, which guarantees that ell is composite. Since ell is odd, it has no divisor equal to 2. The smallest positive divisor of ell strictly greater than 2 must therefore be 3. Thus, the decompwlj weight is exactly:

k = 3

Step 4: Regime boundary evaluation. The threshold boundary between classes evaluates the relation between k^2 and ell. With k = 3, we have k^2 = 9. For all primes p > 11, the offset index satisfies:

ell = p - 2 > 9

Which implies:

k^2 < ell

This strictly satisfies the classification inequality for the Weight Class. The only exception occurs at p = 5 (where ell = 3, yielding k = 3, k^2 = 9 > 3, placing it in the Level Class), and at p = 11 (where ell = 9, yielding k = 3, k^2 = 9 = 9, placing it in the Weight Class at the exact boundary). For all subsequent twin prime pairs, the inequality is strict. Q.E.D.
