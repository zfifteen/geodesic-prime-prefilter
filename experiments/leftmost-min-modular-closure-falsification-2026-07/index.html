Leftmost Min Modular Closure Falsification

Share Core Insight package  ·  Source: x.com/i/grok/share/1f4cf4bbb79542c3af9957e0dd043553  ·  Local transcript: scratch/core_insight_share_transcript.txt  ·  Folder: experiments/leftmost-min-modular-closure-falsification-2026-07/  ·  2026-07-11

Bottom line. The share claimed that picking the leftmost minimum divisor-count interior point (GWR) turns a high remainder-zero count into a gap-closure rule, and that other witness rules lose that power.

invalidated Absolute forcing z(GWR) >= 4 => g = 2 remains dead (PROOF.md Super-Signal; reconfirmed here).

falsified Comparative leftness necessity is dead on p <= 2.5e7: GWR mismatches = 2, rightmost min-tau = 0, first-interior = 152620.

mechanism Both Super-Signal counterexamples are multi-way min-tau ties: leftmost hits z = 4, rightmost hits z = 1. Leftmost bias creates those mismatches on this surface.

1. Observable object

Fix consecutive primes p < q with gap size g = q - p. The interior is the integers strictly between them. Each interior integer n has a divisor count tau(n) and a remainder-zero count z(n) on the fixed modulus vector M_v1 = (2, 3, 5, 7, 30, 210, 2310).

A mismatch is any gap where the chosen witness satisfies z(w) >= 4 while g > 2 (decision failure of "four or more zeros forces a twin gap").

2. Ordinary-language mechanism

Inside a gap, some composites have fewer divisors than others. GWR picks the leftmost interior integer with the smallest divisor count. The share said that particular pick is what makes a high modular zero-count into a reliable "the gap must already be closed" signal, and that alternative picks should produce more (or at least some) mismatches.

What the decisive surface shows instead: when several interiors share the same minimum tau, the leftmost of them can sit on a highly divisible primorial-aligned point while a later min-tau point does not. On the two known Super-Signal false positives, leftmost is the bad modular witness and rightmost is not.

3. Project terms

  • GWR / Leftmost Minimum-Divisor Rule: leftmost interior n achieving min tau.
  • Remainder-zero count z(w): how many moduli in M_v1 divide w.
  • Super-Signal (historical): universal claim z(GWR) >= 4 => g = 2.
  • Selectors: GWR (leftmost min-tau); alt-A (rightmost min-tau); alt-A unique-only (fires only when ties = 1); alt-B (first interior p + 1).

5. Protocol

  1. Build primes and the tau field on a finite interval (field prep only; not inference).
  2. For every consecutive gap with left prime in [11, p_max], compute GWR, alt-A, alt-B (and unique-only arm).
  3. Count z4, twin hits, and mismatches per selector.
  4. Decision rules are pre-registered in HYPOTHESIS.md and probe_selectors.py.

  • Highest consecutive-gap regime: left prime up to 2.5e7. Not 10^18.

  • Classical sieves prepare primes and tau only. They do not choose the selector decision.
