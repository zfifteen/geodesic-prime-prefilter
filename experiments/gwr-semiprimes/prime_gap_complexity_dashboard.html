The Theoretical Engine

Prime boundaries $p$ and $q$ exert localized modulo-2 biases on adjacent composites. Since $p, q > 2$ are odd, $p+1$ and $q-1$ are strictly even, guaranteeing a high concentration of Smallest Prime Factor ($\text{SPF}=2$) composites at the edges. This application performs real-time division and factorization on consecutive prime ranges to prove how this boundary halo generates a U-shaped divisor complexity profile ($\tau(n)$), and demonstrates that this halo decays exponentially inside the gap with characteristic relaxation scale $\lambda$.

U-Shaped Dynamics

This chart maps every composite position linearly between $0$ (right next to boundary $p$) and $1$ (right next to boundary $q$).

  • Small Gaps (g ≤ 20): Strong parabolic U-shape. High boundary density of even numbers overlaps heavily, producing visible low-divisor zones near both sides.
  • Medium Gaps (20 < g < 100): The boundary constraints remain strong, but the center region achieves a higher baseline average.
  • Very Large Gaps (g ≥ 100): The boundary effect fades. The middle section decouples completely, flattening out into the standard density distribution.

Parity Asymmetry Decayed

Since $g$ is always even, the gap contains an odd number of composite items ($g-1$). Both the start ($p+1$) and the end ($q-1$) must be even.

When partitioning into thirds, this guarantees that both the Start and End blocks contain a mathematical bias of even elements, while the Middle block is depleted of them.

Start SPF=2: ~60% - 64%
Middle SPF=2: ~36% - 47%
End SPF=2: ~60% - 62%

As the gap size grows toward infinity, this discrete partitioning boundary bias decreases, and all sections converge asymptotically to $50\%$.

GWR (Gap Winner Rule) Mechanics

The Gap Winner Rule defines the "witness" as the leftmost integer inside a prime gap that achieves the absolute minimal divisor count $\tau(n)$ in that specific gap.

  • Offset +2 Dominance: Since $p$ is prime, $p+1$ is even, making it composite (with $\tau(p+1) \ge 4$). The offset $p+2$ is odd, and if it is semiprime, it achieves $\tau(p+2) = 4$, which often marks the minimum possible divisor count within the entire gap.
  • Semiprime Witnesses (~71-73%): Most GWR witnesses are products of exactly two primes ($pq$). This confirms that the search for the simplest composites concentrates heavily on semiprimes.

Decay of Boundary Parity Constraint & Exponential Fit

Decay Length Definition

The boundary influence manifests as a localized parity constraint. We isolate the decay profile by measuring the absolute difference between average complexity $\tau(x)$ and the asymptotic baseline $M$ for offsets $x = 1, 2, \dots$ across wide gaps:

S(x) = A · e-x/λ + C

By executing a logarithmic linear regression on the boundary offset deviation, we compute the characteristic relaxation scale $\lambda$ dynamically.

A lower $\lambda$ represents a rapidly decaying modular constraint, confirming that the boundary influence vanishes quickly inside large prime gaps.
